I

Estate Preservation

At death, most of what a Canadian owns is treated for tax purposes as if it had been sold that day. Gains on a cottage, a non-registered portfolio or shares of a private company can become taxable at once. Registered plans are generally included in income unless they pass to a surviving spouse, and in Ontario, estate administration tax applies to assets that pass through the will.

The bill is real, it falls due within months, and it often lands on the assets your family most wants to keep. Permanent life insurance can be structured to provide cash at exactly that moment — so the cottage stays in the family and the business is not sold to pay the tax.

  • How do we keep a family cottage or business intact for the next generation?
  • How do we treat children fairly when one inherits the business and the others do not?
  • Which assets can pass outside the will, and how?
II

Corporate Wealth Strategies

If your practice or business is incorporated, surplus earnings tend to accumulate inside the corporation. Drawing them out is taxed personally; leaving them invested produces passive income that is taxed at high rates and, above a threshold, can reduce access to the small business tax rate.

A corporately owned, exempt life insurance policy is paid for with corporate dollars, and its growth is generally not taxed as it accrues. When the death benefit is received, the amount in excess of the policy’s adjusted cost basis is credited to the Capital Dividend Account and can generally be paid to shareholders as tax-free capital dividends.

Questions we help you answer

  • How much of my corporate surplus will I actually spend in my lifetime?
  • What is the most efficient way to move what I do not need to my family?
  • How is passive income inside my corporation affecting my tax rate?
  • Should the corporation, or should I personally, own the policy?
III

Income & Enterprise Protection

For most professionals, the greatest asset is the ability to earn. Disability and critical illness insurance protect your income and savings if illness or injury interrupts your career; disability benefits are generally received tax-free when you pay the premiums personally.

For owners and partners, key-person and buy-sell coverage keep the enterprise standing — funding a buy-out at a fair value, on agreed terms, instead of forcing a sale at the worst possible time.

Questions we help you answer

  • If I could not work for a year, how long would my savings last?
  • What happens to my share of the practice or company if I, or a partner, can no longer work?
  • Is my group or association coverage enough — and what happens if I leave?
  • How do we fund a buy-sell agreement without straining the business?
IV

Retirement Income Design

Participating whole life insurance builds cash value on a tax-advantaged basis, with policy dividends that smooth returns over time rather than following the market day to day. Dividends are not guaranteed.

For some clients, that cash value becomes a stable layer beneath retirement income, or — through an insured retirement strategy — collateral for a loan in later years. These strategies are not right for everyone and carry real risks, including interest-rate, loan and policy-performance risk, which we model and explain in writing before you decide.

Questions we help you answer

  • How do I create retirement income that does not depend on selling investments in a down market?
  • Can a policy’s cash value support retirement while preserving an estate?
  • What are the risks of borrowing against a policy, and when does it make sense?
